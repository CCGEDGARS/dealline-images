The method

Where the Hero's Journey comes from.

The oldest story in the world. Every culture tells it. Somebody is called to something bigger than their ordinary life, doubts it, finds mentors and allies, faces trials, and comes back changed. It works in myths and in films. It works in sales teams too.

1949

Joseph Campbell

The American mythologist studied stories from across the world and found one pattern running through them. In The Hero with a Thousand Faces he called it the monomyth: the call, the refusal, the mentor, the trials, the ordeal and the return. Screenwriters have built films on it ever since.

2009

Robert Dilts and Stephen Gilligan

Two of the leading figures in NLP and coaching turned Campbell's map into a practical method for personal and professional change, set out in their book The Hero's Journey: A Voyage of Self-Discovery. A way to move through doubt, find your resources, and act on a calling.

Today

Why it works in sales

People don't change because they're told to. They change when they see themselves in a story worth finishing. A target is a number. A journey is something you want to complete. And it repeats: every new goal starts a new journey, and every time the hero is stronger.

The 6-hour event

Less lecture. More practice.

Six working hours, plus breaks and lunch. Up to 20 participants per group. Built on your company's real goal and your team's real rejections. Every block uses named tools your team can recognise, repeat and use again on Monday.

0:00
The call

Where does the goal come from? The owner or manager tells the story. Who set it. Why it matters. The numbers behind it. What the team is expected to deliver. Then the goal barometer: three anonymous votes. Do I believe this goal? Do I have the resources? Do I believe in my team? The scores go on the screen.

The toolkit

Strategies that work on Monday morning.

No theory to admire and forget. Every participant gets Edgars' most powerful mental strategies and sales instruments. Tested in more than 15,000 real sales meetings, and ready to use on the very next call.

The inner game

Mental strategies

  • State reset: the right inner state before a difficult call, in under a minute
  • Reframing: a "no" becomes information instead of a wound
  • Limiting belief change: find the belief that holds you back, and replace it
  • Well-formed outcome: turn the company goal into a personal goal you can see and measure
  • Neurological Levels · Robert Dilts: align purpose, identity, beliefs and daily actions with the goal
  • Disney Strategy · Robert Dilts: dreamer, realist, critic: turn doubt into a workable plan
  • Towards and away-from motivation: know what really drives you, and use it

The outer game

Sales instruments

  • The Deal Line: seven steps of a sales meeting, always in the right order
  • Rapport creation: trust in the first minutes, through verbal and nonverbal communication
  • Customer Map: the questions that let the customer convince themselves
  • Time Looping: objection handling, plus 120 techniques for your team's real objections
  • DISC behaviour styles: recognise each type of customer, and how to deal with each one
  • Offer positioning with FAB and USP: why your offer is the clear choice for this customer
  • The three buying motives: earn money, save money, enjoy the process
  • Story-based selling: customers buy meaning, not products
